How much you can put in an HSA this year (2026)

Pick who is on the health plan and whether you are 55 or older this year. We look up the official yearly dollar. Not tax advice.

Who is on the plan and whether you are 55 or older are required.

Just you means only you are covered (the IRS calls this self-only). You and family means a spouse or dependents are on the same plan (the IRS calls this family). Those are the two official yearly caps.
If you are 55 or older at the end of the year, the IRS lets you put in an extra $1,000. That extra is from IRS Publication 969.

What this number is

  • This is how much you can put in an HSA this year.
  • Just you is $4,400. You and family is $8,750. Those are the official 2026 yearly caps.
  • If you are 55 or older at the end of the year, you may add $1,000. That extra is from IRS Publication 969. The example there uses 2025 ($4,300 + $1,000 = $5,300). For 2026 the extra is the same $1,000 on top of $4,400 or $8,750.
  • To use an HSA, the health plan has to have a yearly deductible of at least $1,700 (just you) or $3,400 (you and family). Yearly out-of-pocket costs — deductibles, copays, and other amounts, not premiums — cannot go past $8,500 (just you) or $17,000 (you and family). Those are the plan rules that have to be true, not a second contribution limit.
  • We do not have official dollars here for mid-year coverage changes, more than one HSA, or a different kind of HRA listed in the same IRS document.

This is not tax advice.
